GLM Distribution Explorer

For people who use R but are not sure which GLM distribution to try. You do not need to know the formulas.

In short: load a CSV, check the suggested roles, run, look at ΔAIC and the Q-Q plots, then copy the code of the model you want to work on.

One line of R, piece by piece

m <- glm(duration ~ log(freq) * group + length,
         family = Gamma(link = "log"), data = d)
PieceMeaning
m <-stores the result under the name m
glm( )fits a generalized linear model

link = "log"the effect of X multiplies the mean

In words: "duration is explained by log frequency, group, their interaction, and length, assuming Gamma scatter and multiplying effects."

Change one piece, get another model. The app does this step for you by trying the candidates side by side:

In the app: duration = Y, freq = X numeric (log), group = X categorical, length = X numeric. The interaction tick box adds every pair, so length would interact too; remove what you do not need in the copied code.

1. Check the shape of your data

The app expects one row per observation. If each person answered several items, each answer is its own row (long format):

participantconditionrt
P01A512
P01B634
P02A478

If items are spread over columns, the app usually notices and offers a "Convert to long" button. In Excel, use Save As → CSV UTF-8.

2. Check the roles

Roles are suggested automatically. These questions help you check them:

QuestionRole
What did I measure?Y (outcome), one column
What do I expect to influence it? Is it a group or condition?X categorical
Is it an amount, such as age?X numeric, or X numeric (log) for word frequency and similar
Did the same person, class or item appear several times?Random effect for that ID column
Did observation time or opportunity differ between rows, for a count Y?Exposure
For successes out of several tries: the success columnY, plus the failure column as Failures or the total as Trials
Row numbers, memos, dates, other outcomesNot used

The one to look at twice: if the same person answered more than once and the ID is not a random effect, p-values tend to come out too small.

Copying rows to balance groups: resampling 35 people up to 112 leaves the mean about the same but makes p-values too small, because the same people are counted several times. Unequal group sizes are usually fine as they are. To match a population (for example by gender or age), weights, such as in the R package survey, or adding those variables as X are common options.

Milliseconds stored as whole numbers are still a time, so "Positive continuous" usually suits them.

4. Run and look at two things

  1. ΔAIC: 0 is the best of this list. Up to 2 is practically a tie.
  2. Diagnostics: ✓, △ or ✕. A low ΔAIC with ✕ means "best of the list, but still does not fit well".

What is AIC? A score for how well a model describes the data, with a penalty for every extra parameter. A model with more parameters almost always follows the data more closely, so the penalty keeps it from winning just by being complicated. Lower is better.

ΔAIC is the gap to the lowest one, and the gap is what matters:

ModelAICΔAICIn words
Gamma1200.00lowest here
Lognormal1201.51.5practically a tie
Gaussian1238.038clearly behind
  • The size of AIC itself (1200 or −50) has no meaning. Negative values are fine.
  • AIC can be compared only for the same Y on the same rows. A model for log(Y) and one for raw Y cannot be compared directly; the app corrects this for Lognormal.
  • It ranks this list only. If every candidate is poor, something still comes first, which is why the diagnostics matter.

AICc is AIC adjusted for small samples. BIC penalizes extra parameters more strongly unless the sample is very small. If they point to different models, the candidates are probably close.

5. Read the Q-Q plots

Points on the diagonal are good. The gray band is what chance allows. Both ends often drift a little, so start with the middle.

0/1 data (binomial) are different. Their ordinary residuals pile up at two ends and never look normal, even when the model is right. A histogram with two humps, an odd-looking qqnorm plot or a significant shapiro.test is expected there and does not mean the model is wrong. This app uses randomized quantile residuals, but with single 0/1 answers even those reveal little.

For other data too, shapiro.test becomes significant for tiny deviations once there are many rows. The plots usually tell you more. For Gamma, Poisson and so on, normal raw residuals are not expected either.

6. If it is a tie, or nothing fits

  • Tie: the one that is common in your field or easier to explain is a reasonable choice.
  • Nothing fits: look for a forgotten ID, a missing X, typing errors or extreme values in Y, or a curved relationship.
  • Did not converge: try standardizing numeric X, dropping the random slope, or using fewer X.
  • Separation: an estimate such as 39 with a standard error of 969 usually means that some level of X has only one kind of Y (all 0 or all 1). In R, table(d$x, d$y) shows it. Merging levels, or a penalized method such as Firth's, are common ways forward.
  • NA coefficients in R: some combination of X levels has no data, or one X can be worked out from the others.

7. Put the coefficients into words

After "Choose this" you see a table. For a categorical X, each row compares one level with the reference level (the alphabetically first).

ModelExampleIn words
GaussianconditionB estimate 72B is 72 units higher than A on average
Gamma, Poisson, NB (log)conditionB exp 1.13The mean for B is about 1.13 times A (13% higher)
LognormalconditionB exp 1.13The geometric mean for B is about 1.13 times A
Binomial (logit)conditionB exp 2.0The odds for B are about twice those for A (not "twice as likely")
Ordinal (logit)uinew estimate 0.9"new" shifts answers toward higher categories; exp(0.9) ≈ 2.5 is an odds ratio
  • If the 95% interval excludes 0 (or 1 for exp), the data are hard to reconcile with "no difference". If it includes it, that is not proof of no difference.
  • With random effects, log- and logit-link effects describe comparisons at the same random-effect level (for example the same person).
  • With interactions, a single row no longer means "the overall effect" (see 9).
  • With Gamma (inverse), a positive coefficient means a lower mean (see 8).

8. Gamma: log or inverse link

The distribution is Gamma in both cases. The link decides how X changes the mean.

Gamma (log)Gamma (inverse)
What adds uplog of the mean1 / mean
Effect of Xmultiplies the meanadds to 1 / mean. For a time, that is a speed
Positive coefficienthigher meanlower mean (the sign looks reversed)
How to reportexp(estimate) is a ratio of meanspredicted means are easier than the coefficient
Predicted meanalways positivecan break down when 1 / mean reaches 0 or below

Say a task takes 500 ms in A and 625 ms in B:

LinkCoefficient for BReading
log0.223exp(0.223) = 1.25: B takes 1.25 times as long
inverse−0.00041/500 = 0.0020 becomes 1/625 = 0.0016: speed drops from 2.0 to 1.6 per second

With a numeric X (for example difficulty 0, 1, 2) the two links start to bend differently:

Difficultyloginverse
0500 ms500 ms (2.0 per second)
1625 ms (× 1.25)625 ms (1.6 per second)
2781 ms (× 1.25)833 ms (1.2 per second)
  • With a single categorical X and no random effects, both links give the same predicted means and the same AIC. They differ with numeric X, several X without all interactions, or random effects.
  • Log is a common choice and easier to report. Inverse can suit a question about speed or rate.
  • In R, Gamma() without a link means inverse. Writing Gamma(link = "log") avoids surprises.
  • Gamma (identity) adds directly to the mean in the original unit, but predictions may fall below 0.
  • In the app, tick "Also compare links" to see inverse and identity.

9. Interactions

An interaction means the effect of one X depends on another X. Suppose a score was measured with two tools, for experts and novices, and the averages are:

tool Atool BB − A
expert89+1
novice1014+4

Tool B adds 1 for experts but 4 for novices. The effect differs, so the lines are not parallel:

With a Gaussian model the table would read:

RowEstimateIn words
(Intercept)8expert with tool A (both references)
toolB1effect of B for experts only
groupnovice2novice vs expert with tool A only
toolB:groupnovice3how much larger the effect of B is for novices

Novice with B: 8 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 14. The interaction 3 is the difference of the differences, 4 − 1.

  • Easy to misread: with an interaction in the model, toolB is no longer the overall effect of B. It is the effect at the reference level of the other X.
  • Under a log link, exp(interaction) is a ratio of ratios. If B is 1.1 times A for experts and 1.4 times A for novices, it is 1.4 / 1.1 ≈ 1.27. Lines that are not parallel on the raw scale can be parallel on the log scale.
  • With a numeric X in the interaction, the other effect is read where that X is 0. Standardizing makes 0 its average.
  • It fits questions about whether effects differ. "Include two-way interactions" adds every pair, which can be too many for small data.

4. 실행한 뒤 두 가지 보기

  1. ΔAIC: 0이 후보 중 가장 좋은 값이고, 2까지는 사실상 동률입니다.
  2. 진단: ✓, △, ✕로 표시됩니다. ΔAIC가 낮은데 ✕라면 "후보 중에는 낫지만 잘 맞지는 않을 수 있다"는 뜻입니다.

AIC란? 모델이 데이터를 얼마나 잘 설명하는지에, 모수가 늘어날 때마다 벌점을 더한 점수입니다. 모수가 많은 모델은 거의 언제나 데이터를 더 가깝게 따라가므로, 복잡하다는 이유만으로 유리해지지 않도록 벌점을 줍니다. 낮을수록 좋습니다.

ΔAIC는 가장 낮은 값과의 차이이고, 의미가 있는 것은 이 차이입니다.

모델AICΔAIC말로 풀면

Lognormal1201.51.5사실상 동률
Gaussian1238.038뚜렷하게 뒤처짐
  • AIC 값 자체의 크기(1200이나 −50)에는 뜻이 없습니다. 음수여도 괜찮습니다.
  • 같은 행의 같은 Y로 만든 모델끼리 비교할 수 있습니다. log(Y) 모델과 원래 Y 모델은 바로 비교하기 어려운데, Lognormal은 앱이 보정해 둡니다.
  • 후보 안에서의 순위입니다. 모두 잘 맞지 않아도 1등은 나오기 때문에 진단을 함께 봅니다.

AICc는 표본이 작을 때를 보정한 AIC입니다. BIC는 표본이 아주 작지 않으면 모수에 벌점을 더 크게 줍니다. 서로 다른 모델을 가리키면 후보들이 비슷하다는 신호일 수 있습니다.

5. Q-Q 플롯 읽기

점이 대각선에 붙어 있을수록 좋습니다. 회색 띠는 우연히 벗어날 수 있는 범위입니다. 양 끝은 조금 벗어나는 일이 흔하므로 가운데부터 보시면 됩니다.

0/1 자료(이항)는 조금 다릅니다. 모델이 맞아도 보통의 잔차는 양 끝에 몰리고 정규분포처럼 보이지 않습니다. 두 봉우리 히스토그램, 이상한 qqnorm 그림, 유의한 shapiro.test는 흔한 결과이며 모델이 틀렸다는 뜻으로 보기는 어렵습니다. 이 앱은 무작위화 분위수 잔차를 쓰지만, 0/1 응답 하나하나에서는 이것으로도 드러나는 것이 적습니다.

다른 자료에서도 행이 많으면 shapiro.test는 작은 어긋남에 유의하게 나옵니다. 보통은 그림이 더 많은 것을 알려 줍니다. Gamma, Poisson 등에서도 원래 잔차가 정규분포일 필요는 없습니다.

6. 동률이거나 맞는 분포가 없을 때

  • 동률: 분야에서 흔히 쓰거나 설명하기 쉬운 쪽도 무난한 선택입니다.
  • 맞는 분포가 없을 때: 빠뜨린 ID, 빠진 X, Y의 입력 오류나 극단값, 곡선 관계가 있는지 살펴보실 수 있습니다.
  • 수렴하지 않을 때: 수치 X 표준화, 랜덤 기울기 빼기, X 줄이기를 시도해 보실 수 있습니다.
  • 분리(separation): 추정치 39에 표준오차 969처럼 나오면 X의 어떤 수준에서 Y가 한쪽 값(모두 0 또는 모두 1)뿐인 경우가 많습니다. R에서 table(d$x, d$y)로 확인하실 수 있습니다. 수준을 합치거나 Firth 방법 같은 벌점 추정을 쓰기도 합니다.
  • R에서 계수가 NA일 때: X 수준의 어떤 조합에 데이터가 없거나, 한 X가 다른 X로부터 정해지는 경우일 수 있습니다.

7. 계수를 말로 풀기

"이 분포로 확정"을 누르면 표가 나옵니다. 범주 X의 각 행은 기준 수준(알파벳순 첫 번째)과 비교한 값입니다.

둘 다 분포는 Gamma입니다. 링크는 X가 평균을 어떻게 바꾸는지를 정합니다.

9. 교호작용

교호작용은 한 X의 효과가 다른 X에 따라 달라지는 것입니다. 숙련자(expert)와 초보자(novice)가 두 도구를 쓸 때 점수 평균이 다음과 같다고 하겠습니다.

tool Atool BB − A
expert89+1
novice1014+4

도구 B를 쓰면 숙련자는 1, 초보자는 4가 늘어납니다. 효과가 서로 달라서 두 선이 평행하지 않습니다.

Gaussian 모델이라면 표는 이렇게 나옵니다.

행추정치말로 풀면
(Intercept)8expert가 A를 쓸 때 (둘 다 기준 수준)
toolB1expert에서만 본 B의 효과
groupnovice2A를 쓸 때만 본 novice와 expert의 차이
toolB:groupnovice3novice에서 B의 효과가 얼마나 더 큰지

초보자가 B를 쓸 때: 8 + 1 + 2 + 3 = 14. 교호작용 3은 차이의 차이, 즉 4 − 1입니다.

  • 오해하기 쉬운 부분: 교호작용이 들어가면 toolB는 B의 전체 효과가 아니라, 다른 X가 기준 수준일 때의 효과입니다.
  • 로그 링크에서 exp(교호작용)은 배수의 비입니다. B가 숙련자에서 A의 1.1배, 초보자에서 1.4배라면 1.4 / 1.1 ≈ 1.27입니다. 원래 척도에서 평행하지 않은 선이 로그 척도에서는 평행할 수도 있습니다.
  • 수치 X와의 교호작용이면 다른 X의 효과는 그 X가 0일 때의 값입니다. 표준화하면 0이 평균을 뜻하게 됩니다.
  • 효과가 서로 다른지가 연구 질문일 때 어울립니다. "2원 교호작용 포함"은 모든 쌍을 넣기 때문에 데이터가 작으면 많을 수 있습니다.
